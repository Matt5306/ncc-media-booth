Connect + Confirm Control
Goal: Your laptop can talk to the X32 over Ethernet (OSC).
  1. Plug laptop Ethernet into the same network as the X32.
  2. Ensure your laptop IP is on the same subnet as the X32 (e.g., 10.0.0.x / 255.255.255.0).
  3. Open the dashboard and hit Health Check.
  4. If it fails, check: cable, switch, laptop IP, Wi-Fi interference.
Backup + Restore Points
Treat Scene 99 like a "known-good restore point."
  1. One-time: Save your stable state into Scene 99 named SAFETY_NET.
  2. Before changes: Save current state to a scene or export Show to USB.
  3. If something breaks: Click PANIC RESTORE to recall Scene 99.
EQ Fix: Muddy Sound
Common on vocals, guitars
What it sounds like: Thick, unclear, "woolly" - hard to hear words clearly.
  1. Go to the EQ tab in the dashboard.
  2. Select the channel and choose "Muddy" from Quick Fixes.
  3. This cuts around 300Hz with a moderate Q.
  4. Listen - if still muddy, consider also enabling HPF at 100Hz.
Why this works: 200-400Hz is where mud accumulates, especially in small rooms.
EQ Fix: Harsh Sound
Common on vocals, cymbals
What it sounds like: Piercing, fatiguing, makes you want to turn it down.
  1. Go to the EQ tab and select the channel.
  2. Choose "Harsh" from Quick Fixes.
  3. This cuts around 2-4kHz - the ear fatigue zone.
  4. Be gentle - too much cut makes things dull.
Why this works: 2-4kHz is where the ear is most sensitive.
EQ Fix: Thin Sound
Common on DI guitars, weak vocals
What it sounds like: Lacking body, weak, "small" sounding.
  1. Go to the EQ tab and select the channel.
  2. Choose "Thin" from Quick Fixes.
  3. This boosts around 200Hz to add body.
  4. Don't overdo it - too much causes mud.
EQ Fix: Sibilant Vocals (Harsh S Sounds)
Common with condenser mics
What it sounds like: "Sss" and "Ttt" sounds are piercing and spitty.
  1. Go to the EQ tab and select the vocal channel.
  2. Choose "Sibilant" from Quick Fixes.
  3. This cuts around 5-6kHz with a narrow Q.
  4. If it persists, consider adding a DeEsser in an FX slot.
EQ Fix: Nasal/Honky Sound
What it sounds like: Like talking through your nose, or through a telephone.
  1. Go to the EQ tab and select the channel.
  2. Choose "Nasal" from Quick Fixes.
  3. This cuts around 800-1000Hz.
Why this works: 800-1200Hz is the "telephone" frequency range.
HPF: When and How to Use It
What it does: Removes frequencies below a set point (usually 80-120Hz).
Enable on all vocals Enable on guitars Don't use on kick/bass
  1. Vocals: HPF at 100-120Hz removes rumble and plosives.
  2. Acoustic guitar: HPF at 80Hz removes body resonance.
  3. Electric guitar: HPF at 100Hz removes amp hum.
  4. Kick/Bass: Leave HPF off or very low (30-40Hz) - you need the low end!
Effects: Choosing the Right Reverb
Reverb adds space and depth. Different types for different uses:
HallLarge space, 2-4 sec decay. Good for worship, ballads.
PlateSmooth, bright. The go-to for vocals. 1-2 sec.
RoomSmall, natural. Good for drums, subtle ambience. 0.5-1 sec.
ChamberWarm, musical. Good for acoustic instruments.
Tip: Use the FX presets in the Effects tab for good starting points.

  2. Set delay time from the table above.
  3. Keep feedback low (2-3 repeats) for most worship uses.
  4. Use high-cut on the delay to keep it from getting harsh.
Effects: How FX Sends Work
Understanding the FX bus system:
  1. FX Bus: A dedicated bus that feeds an effect processor.
  2. Send Level: How much of each channel goes to the FX bus.
  3. Return: The processed effect comes back on the FX Return channel.
Example: To add reverb to vocals:
  • Reverb is loaded in FX Slot 1, fed by Bus 7
  • Increase the vocal channel's send to Bus 7
  • More send = more reverb on that vocal
Troubleshoot: Feedback on a Vocal Mic
Most common in monitors
  1. Fast safe step: Lower the monitor send a little.
  2. Mute FX returns temporarily (use Quick Ops tab).
  3. Ensure HPF is enabled (100Hz for vocals).
  4. Check mic position - don't aim at wedges.
  5. If still ringing: use EQ to cut the ringing frequency.
Troubleshoot: No Sound from Bluetooth
  1. Confirm the phone is connected and playing audio.
  2. Check the Bluetooth channels (Aux 5/6) - unmuted, faders up.
  3. Confirm routing to Main LR.
  4. For livestream: check broadcast bus routing too.
Troubleshoot: No Sound from Computer/USB
  1. Check input source selection (USB vs Local).
  2. Check mutes and faders on playback channels.
  3. Confirm routing to Main LR.
  4. Check USB/card routing in the X32 setup menu.
Troubleshoot: Livestream Mix Problems
  1. Identify: is livestream fed from a Bus or Matrix?
  2. Check meters on that bus - is there signal?
  3. Verify Pastor and Vocals are sent to the broadcast feed.
  4. If quiet but meters look OK: check encoder/PC input gain.
Training: Using Training Mode
Training Mode lets you practice without affecting the actual mixer.
  1. Click Enter Training Mode on the left panel.
  2. Try any operation - it will be logged but NOT sent to the X32.
  3. Review the Action Log to see what would have happened.
  4. Click Exit Training Mode when ready to make real changes.
Use this for: New volunteer training, practicing EQ changes, learning the system.
